Guestbookfree

Visitors leave a name and one line, in handwriting. Nothing shows until you approve it — the pile waits in your dashboard.

saw you at urban spree. unreal set.— peachfound this at 3am and i’m not leaving.— a stranger

Alivefreelifetime

“3 people here right now”, live. A clock that says it’s 3:12 am where you are. And a hit counter from a better decade — odometer wheels, an LCD, split-flap cards.

3 people here right nowit’s 3:12 am in berlin012412

Secret wordlifetime

A link that isn’t on the page until someone types the word — anywhere on it. Neither the word nor the link sits in the page’s source.

night busthe b-side, unreleased

Ask me anythingfree

Visitors leave a question. It goes to you and nowhere else — answering is what puts it on the page, their question and your answer in your handwriting. Never answer it and nobody ever sees it.

? best show you’ve playeda laundrette in leipzig, eleven people

A chalkboardfree

A small board on your page. People draw on it with a finger; you keep the ones you like. Strokes only — no uploads, no images, nothing to moderate but a drawing.

kept 12 · binned 3

The tallyfree

The ask box is questions coming in. This is one going out: ask something, give two to four answers, and everyone who visits picks one. The count is scratched on your card in tally marks — nobody's name, just how it's going.

13 · the loud one

A vigilfree

A shelf of candles. A visitor lights one — no name, no words, nothing to moderate — and it burns down and goes out on its own. Which means an empty shelf is honest: nobody has been by in a while.

6 burning · out after 12h

The moonfree

Tonight's real moon, in the corner of your card. Not a picture of a moon — the actual phase, worked out from the date, drawn as one shape. Nothing fetched, nothing stored, and the crescent is on the correct side, which is the bit most moons on the internet get wrong.

waxing gibbous · 78% lit

Neighboursfree

Name up to five other misa.lol pages. A name does nothing on its own — it shows up only if that page names you back. So nobody can put themselves next to you and you can't put yourself next to anybody: it takes two. A webring, basically, one pair at a time.

peachvoidlune

The archivefree

Every time you save, the page you just replaced goes on a shelf — the last twelve. Break something at 3am and you can put any of them back, and putting one back is itself undoable. Switch it on and visitors can read your page backwards too.

12 keptyou’re reading this page as it was on 3 june.

The night shiftlifetime

Give your page a night. Any block you mark night only is on it between hours you pick — your hours, not the visitor's — and outside them it isn't in the page at all, so nobody reads it early from the source. While it's on, the page says why there's more here.

11 pm → 5 amit’s 3:14 am where ren is. some of this is only here now.

The drawlifetime

Write a small deck — a line each, up to twelve. Every visitor is dealt one of them and keeps it for the day; the person next to them gets a different one. Nothing about who got what is stored: it's worked out fresh, on the page, every time.

go outsidethe b-side is better and you know it

The other sidelifetime

Your page can have a back. There's a folded corner at the bottom of the card — turn it over and the whole thing rolls round to a second face: something handwritten, and any block you send there. Hover the little card.

renliner notesa second face, same card

Time capsulelifetime

Write something now that your page opens on a day you choose. Until then it’s a wax seal and a countdown — and the words are not in the page: the server holds them until the day, so nobody can read ahead.
