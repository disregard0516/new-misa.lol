      What shows

      “Remember visitors” writes nothing to our servers: a returning visitor’s own browser keeps the count and shows them a line — you’ve been here before, and what turned up while they were away: 2 new answers, 1 new drawing since, with a small mark on each one. Clearing their site data forgets it, and you never see who they are or what they’ve seen.

      Things a page shouldn't be able to do.

      A word that unlocks a link. Weather from where you are, drawn over the page. Words sealed until a day you pick. Parts that only exist after dark. A line dealt to each visitor. A whole other side. None of this is necessary. That's rather the point.

      Secret word lifetime perk

      A link that isn’t on the page until someone types the word anywhere on it — or taps your avatar five times and says it. Neither the word nor the link sits in the page’s source.

      The moon

      Tonight's real moon, in the corner of your card — the actual phase, worked out from the date. Nothing is fetched and nothing is stored; the moon is a clock everybody already shares. It's a crescent on the correct side, which is the bit most drawings of the moon get wrong.

      The sky lifetime perk

      Your page wears the weather where you are. Raining in berlin? It rains on your page — snow, fog, storms with a flash, stars on a clear night. Read from the same free forecast as the weather block, checked every ten minutes, and it holds still for anyone who asks for less motion.

      The night shift lifetime perk

      Give your page a night. Any content block marked night only is on it between these hours — your hours, not the visitor's — and outside them it isn't in the page at all, so nobody can read it early by looking at the source. While it's on, the page says so: it's 3:14 am where you are. some of this is only here now.

      The draw lifetime perk

      Write a small deck — one line a line, up to twelve. Every visitor is dealt one of them and keeps it for the day; the person next to them gets a different one. Nothing about who got what is stored anywhere: it's worked out from the same anonymous shape the view counter uses, fresh, every time the page is drawn.
